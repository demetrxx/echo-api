# Problem

## Framing

This section should not be played as "5 problems".

It should feel like **the path where a thought falls apart**:

```txt
Thought appears -> gets lost -> loses context -> feels hard to shape -> never reaches the world
```

The section should continue the hero visual instead of becoming a generic pain-point grid.

## Main Version

### Heading

**Creating isn't hard because you have nothing to say.**

### Subheading

**It's hard because your thoughts arrive messy, scattered, and unfinished - long before you sit down to write.**

### Problem Cards

#### 1. Ideas arrive at the wrong time

```txt
Your best thoughts don't wait for your writing session.
They show up in the shower, on a walk, in a taxi, between tasks - and disappear just as fast.
```

#### 2. Everything gets scattered

```txt
Voice notes, saved links, random messages to yourself, half-written drafts.
The idea exists somewhere - but finding it again feels like work.
```

#### 3. Raw thoughts stay raw

```txt
You know what you mean, but the thought is not clear enough to publish yet.
Shaping it into something clear takes energy you often don't have.
```

#### 4. You lose direction

```txt
You publish when inspiration hits, then disappear.
Not because you lack discipline - because there's no simple thread connecting your ideas.
```

#### 5. Tools make it heavier

```txt
Most creator tools add calendars, streaks, dashboards, and pressure.
They ask you to become more systematic before helping you create.
```

## Short UI Version

```txt
Ideas come at the wrong time
Your best thoughts appear when you're not ready to write.

They get lost everywhere
Notes, chats, links, drafts - scattered across too many places.

Raw thoughts stay raw
You know what you mean, but shaping it into a post takes effort.

Direction fades
Without a clear thread, consistency becomes random.

Tools add pressure
Streaks, calendars, and dashboards make creation feel like another obligation.
```

## More Emotional Version

```txt
You had the thought.

For a moment, it was clear.
Then it became a note to yourself.
Or a voice note you never opened again.
Or nowhere at all.

And even when you find it later,
it takes energy to remember what it meant,
shape it,
and make it sound like you.

That's where most ideas die.
Not because they were weak.
Because the process around them was.
```
